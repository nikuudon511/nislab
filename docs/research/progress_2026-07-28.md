## 4. Readings

- Unified TTL 0.2/0.2 does not overturn the old Hybrid+RMR result. ORR remains high at 97.78%, within about 1 point of the old 98.6% result.
- Hybrid+RMR keeps both high- and low-priority ORR high. This suggests low-priority V2N2V forwarding is not merely extra traffic; it contributes materially to total ORR.
- High-priority V2N2V only preserves high-priority ORR and high-priority RL almost exactly, while low-priority ORR drops to 47.01%. This supports the claim that high-priority-only forwarding is a valid safety-oriented fallback but not sufficient for overall recognition performance.
- MEC-side RMR only increases high-priority RL>=2 from 9.16% to 25.32%, but ORR collapses to 75.39% and MEC AoI p99 worsens to 9.283 s. The added redundancy is not useful if the MEC path becomes stale.
- V2V-side RMR removal therefore appears to trade freshness and ORR for more repeated updates. For this workload, redundancy alone is not a good target unless AoI and DL capacity are controlled.
- V2N2V only with high-priority-only forwarding still fails to replace Hybrid. Even after removing low-priority V2N2V forwarding, DL required/capacity p99 remains 1.89, MEC update failure is 94.95%, and MEC AoI p99 is 9.846 s.
- The high-priority-only V2N2V result gives direct evidence against the claim that "V2N2V alone is enough." High-priority ORR is only 37.05% and high-priority RL>=2 is 0%, so the V2N2V-only path cannot provide fresh or redundant high-priority recognition under this dense setting.
- V2N2V only with object-limit 10 constrains required DL load to roughly capacity
  (DL req/cap p99 0.97), but recognition remains poor: ORR 27.65%, high ORR
  49.04%, low ORR 0.08%, and high RL>=2 is 0%. The cap removes too much content
  to recover overall recognition.
- V2N2V only with object-limit 20 is not restrictive enough: DL req/cap p99
  remains 1.86, MEC update failure is 100%, and MEC AoI p99 grows to 45.5 s.
  The method therefore falls back to the same downlink fanout bottleneck as
  unconstrained V2N2V.
- Disabling high-priority dual transmission in Hybrid+RMR reduces ORR from
  97.78% to 87.23%, high ORR from 98.78% to 90.45%, and low ORR from 96.43% to
  82.67%. High RL>=2 also falls from 9.16% to 2.13%.
- High-dual disabled also worsens MEC freshness: MEC update failure rises from
  25.50% to 40.53%, MEC AoI p99 rises from 248 ms to 4.79 s, and DL req/cap p99
  rises from 0.90 to 1.90. Therefore high-priority dual TX is not merely extra
  redundancy; it stabilizes the route-control/RMR balance in this setting.
- Clean Hybrid baseline without RMR reaches only ORR 63.30%, high ORR 70.53%,
  and low ORR 53.29%. Compared with Hybrid+RMR, ORR is lower by 34.48 points.
- Hybrid no RMR has high RL>=2 of 25.32%, higher than Hybrid+RMR's 9.16%, but
  MEC update failure is 82.52%, MEC AoI p99 is 25.6 s, and DL req/cap p99 is
  2.34. This is a clear example that repeated updates are not useful when the
  MEC path is overloaded and stale.
- Clean V2V no RMR reaches ORR 52.64%, high ORR 62.33%, and low ORR 39.36%.
  Compared with clean V2V+RMR (ORR 44.25%, high ORR 54.30%, low ORR 30.43%),
  V2V-side RMR lowers recognition in this setting.
- V2V no RMR and V2V+RMR have almost the same mean CBR (0.802) and the same
  V2V required-load p99 ratio (2.13), so CPM object deletion does not relieve
  the sidelink bottleneck enough. It reduces radio loss (63.82% -> 55.74%) but
  also removes useful content/redundancy, increasing update failure and lowering
  ORR.
- Clean V2N2V no RMR reaches only ORR 13.92%, high ORR 24.48%, and low ORR
  0.00%. MEC update failure is 100%, MEC AoI p99 is 84.4 s, and DL req/cap p99
  is 7.38. This cleanly reproduces the V2N2V-only downlink fanout bottleneck
  under TTL 0.2/0.2.
- Compared with V2N2V+object-payload RMR, ORR is unchanged at 13.92%, but DL
  req/cap p99 worsens from 4.73 to 7.38 and AoI p99 worsens from 62.6 s to
  84.4 s. RMR reduces offered load but not enough to make V2N2V-only viable.

## 5. Interpretation

- The best current explanation is that full Hybrid+RMR works because it balances three constraints at once:
  - V2V-side RMR prevents sidelink CPM payload from remaining too heavy under high CBR.
  - MEC-side object-payload RMR keeps downlink required load below capacity.
  - Adaptive low-priority V2N2V forwarding fills recognition gaps that high-priority-only forwarding leaves behind.
- ORR and RL/RV give different messages:
  - ORR shows Hybrid+RMR is currently the best recognition method.
  - RL/RV show high-priority recognition is mostly single-update recognition: high RL>=1 is 98.32%, but high RL>=2 is only 9.16%.
- Future control should not simply maximize repeated transmissions. It should keep MEC AoI and DL load stable while selectively raising high-priority RL when bandwidth is scarce.
- The V2N2V-only high-priority result suggests that reducing content class alone is insufficient. The remaining bottleneck is fanout and freshness: even high-priority-only delivery can exceed downlink capacity because many receivers need timely updates.
- The object-limit 10/20 results show the same tradeoff from a different angle:
  per-CPM content caps can reduce downlink load, but a fixed cap creates a sharp
  capacity/recognition tradeoff. Ten candidates keeps DL load near capacity but
  loses most low-priority and about half of high-priority recognition; twenty
  candidates keeps more candidates but overloads DL and becomes stale.
- The high-dual disabled ablation suggests that high-priority dual transmission
  is part of the current Hybrid+RMR mechanism, not a removable detail. Turning
  it off lowers V2V CBR, but ORR and MEC AoI become worse, indicating that the
  saved sidelink load does not compensate for lost high-priority complementary
  delivery.
- The clean Hybrid no RMR result upgrades the old diagnostic baseline: under the
  same TTL 0.2/0.2 and BG500 setting, adding Hybrid alone does not solve the MEC
  freshness bottleneck. RMR is required to turn V2N2V into a fresh complement to
  V2V rather than stale extra traffic.
- The clean V2V no RMR result clarifies the role of V2V-side RMR. In this
  dense setting, deleting CPM objects inside V2V packets is not equivalent to
  reducing the number of contending sidelink transmissions. It can lower radio
  loss while still reducing ORR because the channel remains busy and fewer
  useful objects/redundant updates are delivered.
- The clean V2N2V no RMR result closes the clean baseline gap for V2N2V-only.
  The method is not limited by RMR design alone; even without deletion, MEC
  fanout requires far more downlink capacity than available, making nearly all
  updates stale.
- This strengthens the Hybrid explanation: V2V is needed for local, low-latency dissemination, while V2N2V should act as a selective complement rather than the sole CPM dissemination path.

## 6. Slide Use

- Main result: Hybrid+RMR under TTL 0.2/0.2 gives ORR 97.78%, High ORR 98.78%, Low ORR 96.43%.
- Ablation result: high-priority-only V2N2V keeps High ORR but sacrifices Low ORR and total ORR.
- Ablation result: MEC-side RMR only raises RL>=2 but makes MEC AoI too stale, so it is not a better solution.
- V2N2V-only high-priority result: high-priority-only filtering does not solve V2N2V downlink fanout; high-priority ORR remains only 37.05%.
- High-dual disabled ablation: removing high-priority dual TX drops ORR by about
  10.5 points and makes MEC AoI p99 much worse, so the current high-priority
  dual forwarding should be presented as an important component of Hybrid+RMR.
- Clean Hybrid no RMR baseline: ORR is only 63.30% and MEC AoI p99 is 25.6 s,
  while Hybrid+RMR reaches ORR 97.78% and AoI p99 248 ms. This supports the
  slide claim that RMR is what makes the V2N2V complement fresh enough.
- Clean V2V no RMR baseline: ORR is 52.64%, while V2V+RMR is 44.25%.
  V2V-side object deletion lowers radio loss but does not reduce CBR or
  required-load p99, so it should be used as evidence that V2V-side RMR alone
  is not a sufficient high-density solution.
- Clean V2N2V no RMR baseline: ORR is 13.92%, MEC fail is 100%, and DL req/cap
  p99 is 7.38. This is the clearest slide evidence against "V2N2V alone is
  enough."
- Suggested slide message: Hybrid+RMR is not only bandwidth addition. It needs both route complementarity and path-specific RMR to preserve freshness and recognition.

## 9. 2026-08-04 Prediction-Control Positioning

- Prediction should be introduced as a timing/stability layer, not as a
  replacement for the current Hybrid+RMR story.  V2N2V/RMR decide which path and
  which object information to use; prediction decides when to start or strengthen
  those controls.
- To isolate the value of prediction, first compare `V2V+RMR current-CBR` and
  `V2V+RMR predicted-CBR`.  This avoids mixing CBR prediction with MEC
  downlink fanout, MEC-side RMR, and high-priority dual transmission.
- The next integrated comparison should be `Hybrid+RMR current-CBR` versus
  `Hybrid+RMR predicted-CBR`.  If prediction improves the integrated method, the
  claim should be phrased as "effective under the evaluated conditions" rather
  than "optimal".
- For integrated prediction, use CBR for V2V/sidelink congestion and a
  downlink load metric equivalent to DL PRB utilization for the V2N2V side.
  This can be treated as a gNB/MEC-side observation, not a vehicle-side
  directly observable metric.
- Expected outcome: average ORR may not increase much because Hybrid+RMR is
  already near 98%.  More plausible gains are lower DL load peaks, lower MEC
  update failure/AoI, and better high-priority RL/RV during congestion peaks.
- Safe paper wording if prediction+Hybrid+RMR is best: "Under the evaluated
  dense-traffic conditions, combining route complementarity, path-specific RMR,
  and load prediction gave the highest recognition performance and the most
  stable high-priority delivery."  Avoid claiming global optimality.

## 10. 2026-08-04 Prediction-Control Implementation Smoke/Trend

Implementation changes:

- `hybrid-v2v-v2n2v` now respects `--predictive-rmr=true`; previously the
  Hybrid method reset predictive RMR to false, so a predicted Hybrid+RMR
  comparison could not actually be run.
- Hybrid route control now uses `max(measured CBR, predicted CBR)` as the
  control input.  This prevents a stale/underestimated prediction from delaying
  MEC activation compared with the current-CBR controller.
- Added an optional MEC-side downlink load prediction input:
  `--predictive-dl-load-control`.  It extrapolates recent
  `mec_dl_required_load_ratio` upward over a short lead time and uses that as
  extra pressure for MEC-side RMR.
- `observation.csv` now includes `mec_dl_predicted_load_ratio_for_rmr`.

Validation:

- Build passed with `CCACHE_DISABLE=1 ./ns3 build`.
- 50-vehicle, 5-second smoke completed for current and predicted Hybrid+RMR.
  Prediction logs were populated and current/predicted results matched after
  applying the `max(current,predicted)` route-control fix.
- 200-vehicle, 30-second Trend was attempted but was too slow for quick
  turnaround; it was stopped after only early rows and should not be used.

Light Trend result:

- Run base: `results/predictive_hybrid_light_trend_50v_30s_20260804_232701`
- Scenario: 50 vehicles, 30 s, BG500-style load, TTL high/low 0.2/0.2.
- Current-CBR Hybrid+RMR:
  - ORR 77.87%, high ORR 81.64%, low ORR 71.75%.
  - MEC update failure 11.72%, MEC AoI p99 201 ms.
  - MEC DL required/cap p99 0.095.
  - high RL>=1 80.36%, high RL>=2 1.09%, high RV mean 0.407.
- Predicted-CBR/DL-load Hybrid+RMR:
  - ORR 80.25%, high ORR 84.61%, low ORR 73.58%.
  - MEC update failure 15.07%, MEC AoI p99 200 ms.
  - MEC DL required/cap p99 0.095.
  - high RL>=1 83.28%, high RL>=2 1.09%, high RV mean 0.422.

Interpretation:

- In the light Trend setting, prediction slightly improved ORR
  (+2.38 points), high ORR (+2.98 points), low ORR (+1.82 points), high
  RL>=1, and high RV.
- The improvement appears to come from earlier/more frequent MEC complement:
  mean dual-route count increased from 33.0 to 35.6 vehicles, and the predicted
  CBR series reached about 0.72 while measured mean CBR peaked around 0.69.
- MEC DL required/capacity p99 stayed almost unchanged and below capacity in
  both runs.  This light setting therefore does not prove DL-load prediction is
  useful under overload; it only validates logging/control plumbing.
- MEC update failure was worse in the predicted run, so prediction is not yet a
  clean win.  The next meaningful run should be a 200-vehicle shorter Candidate
  or a 100-second overnight run if the purpose is slide/paper evidence.
- Treat this result as diagnostic only, not main evidence.

  control timing but also the one-to-many downlink fanout structure.
- For the resume paper, this can support the statement that prediction should
  be treated as a timing/stability layer on top of the hybrid route/RMR design,
  not as a substitute for route complementarity.
